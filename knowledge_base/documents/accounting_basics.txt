Accounting Basics

Accounting is the process of recording, classifying, summarizing, and interpreting financial transactions.

The accounting equation is:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets are resources controlled by a business.
Examples include cash, inventory, equipment, and accounts receivable.

Liabilities are obligations owed by a business.
Examples include accounts payable, loans, and outstanding expenses.

Equity represents the owner's residual interest in the business after liabilities are deducted from assets.

Revenue is income earned from normal business activities.

Expenses are costs incurred to generate revenue.

The double-entry accounting system records the effect of each financial transaction in at least two accounts.

For a valid double-entry transaction:

Total Debits = Total Credits

Debit and Credit

Every accounting transaction affects at least two accounts using the double-entry accounting system.

A debit is recorded on the left side of an account.

A credit is recorded on the right side of an account.

Debits generally increase asset and expense accounts and decrease liability, equity, and revenue accounts.

Credits generally increase liability, equity, and revenue accounts and decrease asset and expense accounts.

For every balanced journal entry:

Total Debits = Total Credits
